# Shadows on the Cave Wall: What Attention Says About Extraction Errors Is Mostly Geometry

Code, pre-registered declarations and measured tables for the article **Shadows on the Cave Wall: What Attention Says About
Extraction Errors Is Mostly Geometry** (Souza, Miranda e Silva, Espíndola and Cerqueira; submitted to *Computational Linguistics*).

This repository exists so that anyone can **redo every verdict** in the article and **trace
where every number came from**. Nothing here was rewritten to fit the article: the
declarations are the signed ones, and the tables are the ones the executor wrote.

> **Language.** The documents at the top level are in English. Code comments, identifiers,
> the declaration documents (`docs/tese/declaracoes/`) and the protocol are in Portuguese,
> the language in which they were written and signed. They are not translated because the
> signed text is what the hashes cover.

## The question

An entity extractor makes confident mistakes. If an internal signal of the model (its
attention, hidden states or output scores) could point to where it is fragile, human
review could be limited to those predictions. The question is whether any such signal
**adds** to the confidence the model already reports, measured against an **exact** null:
the attention mass a span of *k* tokens would receive from a model that distinguishes
nothing is *k*/|K|, which follows from algebra, not from fitting.

## How to verify, in order of cost

| level | what it checks | command | needs |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | the test suite: executor, nulls, contracts between declarations and code | `pytest tests/` | CPU, 10 s |
| 0b | that the declarations, addenda and configurations are the ones deposited on Zenodo before submission | `python tools/registro_publico.py --conferir` | CPU, 1 s |
| 1 | **every verdict** in the article, recomputed from the measured tables and compared cell by cell with the published tables | `python reproduzir/analise.py` | CPU, ~30 min |
| 1b | **every number, table and figure in the text of the article**, regenerated from the level-1 tables and compared byte for byte | `python reproduzir/artigo.py` | CPU, ~30 s |
| 2 | that the measured tables come **from those weights** | `python tools/impressao_digital.py <weights>` and then the measurement (see [REPRODUCE.md](REPRODUCE.md)) | the published weights; a GPU for the decoder |

Level 1 exits with code 1 if any cell differs. Numbers are compared as the same float, not
within a tolerance. [VERIFICATION.md](VERIFICATION.md) records what each level gave when
the authors ran it in a clean environment.

## Limitations an auditor should know

1. **The signing dates cannot be verified by third parties.** The declarations were signed
   in commits of a private development repository. Their hashes are in the documents, and
   the executor refuses to run if a declaration does not match them, but the **date** of
   each signature depends on a history the reader cannot see. What the reader can verify is
   that, given each declaration, the results follow from it, and that no declaration has
   changed since the hashes were deposited on Zenodo (`docs/tese/declaracoes/REGISTRO_PUBLICO.txt`).
2. **decl-07 and decl-08 were signed before, and measured after,** the result of decl-09 in
   the same design was known. The declaration precedes the measurement; ignorance of the
   neighbouring result does not.
3. **One protocol rule changed between versions.** Orienting each score on the calibration
   partition was introduced in version 5. The executor applies to each declaration the rules
   of the version under which it was signed (`tests/test_orientacao_por_versao.py`).
4. **decl-07/08 were measured on CPU; decl-05/06, with the same weights, on GPU.** The
   predictions are identical in all 11,115 rows; confidence differs by at most 3×10⁻⁵.
5. **Corpus text is not distributed.** CoNLL-2003 is Reuters text and requires an agreement
   with NIST. The `mencao` column of the decoder tables is a truncated SHA-256 of the span.
   From level 2 on, the auditor needs their own copy of each corpus.
